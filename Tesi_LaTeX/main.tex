\chapter*{Abstract}

Pulsar timing arrays search for a stochastic gravitational wave background
(GWB) at nanohertz frequencies by monitoring the times of arrival of the
pulses of millisecond pulsars. The detection of the GWB requires an
accurate model of the noise of each pulsar, whose components can be
degenerate with one another. In this thesis we study the noise of
PSR~J1640+2224 in the DR2low dataset, which combines the second data
release of the European Pulsar Timing Array with low-frequency
observations of LOFAR. The analysis is performed with \textsc{Discovery},
a recent code based on JAX, which we first validate against
\textsc{Enterprise}. The posterior of the DM variations has two peaks, and
the contours between the DM and the scattering parameters are L-shaped,
the sign of a degeneracy between the two chromatic processes. This
structure remains when the chromatic index of the scattering variations is
left free and when a band noise is added to the model. We then analyze
simulated datasets. When the data contain only the processes of the model
the degeneracy does not appear, so it is not due to the frequency coverage
of the data. An unmodeled GWB and an unmodeled band noise in the LOFAR
band bias the intrinsic red noise and the DM variations respectively, and
the band noise can remain undetected. None of the simulations however
reproduces the whole structure observed in the real data, whose origin
remains open.
